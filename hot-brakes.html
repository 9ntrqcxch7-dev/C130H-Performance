Brake Energy & Temperature

Enter the landing or rejected‑takeoff conditions at brake application. This reproduces the Figure 9‑9 Sheet 2 → Sheet 5 → Sheet 6 chain: gross weight and speed give an uncorrected kinetic energy per brake, which is then corrected for brake and reverse‑thrust availability, runway temperature, wind, slope and pressure altitude, then converted to a resulting brake temperature.

Conditions
Power‑cut / brake‑application speed — touchdown speed for a landing, or speed at brake application for a rejected takeoff.
Wind: positive = headwind, negative = tailwind.
Slope: positive = uphill, negative = downhill.
Pressure altitude: field elevation corrected for non‑standard altimeter setting — read directly off the altimeter set to 29.92 inHg / 1013.25 hPa, or field elevation ± ≈27 ft per hPa the QNH is below/above standard.
Use ~200°F for a normal taxi‑out; use a cooled‑down/ambient value if brakes have had time to cool, or the result of a prior landing for a subsequent takeoff.

03

Brake Temperature Zones

From SMP 777 Figure 9‑9, Sheet 1 — required actions at each brake temperature band. Applies for all flap configurations under maximum anti‑skid braking.

ZoneRangeRequired actions
Normal 0–1000°F
  • At 512°F+, inspect landing gear and brake areas; request standby fire equipment if leaks are observed.
  • Approach the area with extreme caution if inspection is necessary.
Caution 1000–1150°F
  • Clear the runway. Do not set brakes. Chock the nosewheel.
  • Request standby fire fighting equipment — fire is probable if a hydraulic leak occurs.
  • Evacuate away from the main gear wheels; do not approach until brakes cool to 400°F; no takeoff before inspection.
Caution 1150–1500°F
  • Clear the runway. Do not set brakes. Chock the nosewheel.
  • Request fire fighting equipment — fire and/or blown fuse plugs are probable.
  • Evacuate the immediate vicinity; do not approach until 400°F; no takeoff before inspection.
Danger 1500–1600°F
  • Clear the runway. Do not set brakes. Chock the nosewheel.
  • Request fire fighting equipment — hydraulic fluid fire and blown fuse plugs are imminent.
  • Leave the immediate vicinity; do not approach until 400°F; no takeoff before inspection.

Flight‑test demonstrated limit: 24 million ft·lb per brake, producing brake temperatures up to 1500°F. The characteristics of the braking system beyond either value are not known — exceeding them could mean inability to stop on the runway available, hydraulic fluid fire, or blown fuse plugs.
