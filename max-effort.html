Every curve here was traced by machine from photographs of the SMP 777 nomograms and refitted — it reproduces the paper charts to roughly ±0.5 % on torque and take‑off factor and ±1–3 % on the speeds and distances (see §5). Baseline configuration only: normal bleed, H+ pack off, 1077 °C TIT limit, 100 % engines, 50 % flaps. 01.1

Corrections — Runway & Conditions

Fig 3A‑24 (refusal / critical engine failure speed), Fig 3A‑25 sheets 2‑3 (minimum field length) and Fig 3A‑99 (acceleration check time) all correct off the same handful of physical inputs. Every field below defaults to that chart's own printed baseline — leave everything as‑is and the corrected numbers in §1 equal the uncorrected ones (see the identity check in §5) — except brake pressure, whose two charts print different baselines; see that field's note. Percent flaps is fixed at 50 % (Fig 3A‑24 sheet 1 baseline, and what FOM‑B mandates for max effort) so it is not exposed here.

Braking‑friction scale, not a general condition rating — 23 = dry (baseline), ≈12 ≈ packed snow, ≈5 ≈ wet ice, 2 = glare ice. Lower = more slippery, longer stopping distance.
Extra parasite drag from external stores, open doors, or airframe damage, in equivalent‑percent‑drag units — 0 = clean baseline configuration.
Default 3,000 psi assumes full service. Note the two charts print different baselines for this one field — Fig 3A‑24 (refusal speed) is drawn at a conservative 2,030 psi, Fig 3A‑25 (field length) at 3,000 psi — so at the default, refusal speed still picks up its deserved credit while field length is unaffected. See §5.
Runway Surface Condition depth code for standing slush or water, 0 (dry, baseline) to 10 (deepest charted) — adds drag and cuts braking the same way a low RCR does.
Fig 3A‑25 only — extra margin added to Vr/Vto by choice.

02

Speed Ladder & Briefing Card

The Check Time / Speed table of FOM‑B 4.16.1.1, laid out in the order the crew will call it. Vrot is the one field the lathund leaves to the crew — it is pre‑filled with Vto − 5 KIAS, the SMP 777 rotation convention, and is yours to raise per FOM‑B step 6. Note that for a maximum effort take‑off SMP 777 does not floor Vto at VMCA as it does for a normal take‑off: you lift off below VMCA and accelerate through it (FOM‑B step 9), which is exactly why VMCA is on the card.

Crew‑briefed
Default Vto − 5. FOM‑B 4.16.1.1 step 6: “Beakta möjligheten att höja Vrot med hänsyn till banlängd och dylikt.” Reset to the computed default with the button below.

    05

    Digitization Accuracy

    SMP 777 presents all of this as hand‑plotted nomograms, not equations. Each curve family was traced from a 400 dpi render of the manual page, refitted to a smooth function, and then checked back against direct readings of the same chart and — where the manual provides one — against its own worked example. Here is how far off each one is, including the one case that does not reconcile.

    05.1

    What is not implemented

    Not in this appChartWhat you must still do by hand
    Bleed / engine configuration Fig 3A‑6 sheets 2–6, Fig 3A‑7, 3A‑9 Only sheet 1 (torque for 100 % engines, normal bleed) is digitized. For 98 % or 95 % engines, all‑bleed‑on or bleeds‑closed, use the paper sheets — the torque and the take‑off factor both change.
    Non‑baseline TIT limit / H+ pack / generators Fig 3A‑6 & 3A‑8 carry‑across grids Both charts carry lower guide grids for TIT limit (baseline 1077 °C), H+ pack (baseline OFF), % engines (baseline 100) and generators (baseline 40/60). This app computes the baseline case only; any other setting shifts the answer along those guide lines.
    1/√σ on Fig 3A‑99 Fig 3A‑99, third grid Wind and runway‑slope corrections in §1.1 are traced from the chart. The 1/√σ column's guide lines were too close to vertical to trace reliably at any resolution tried, so that step is applied as a direct multiply by the SMOE value computed in §1 instead of a traced curve — matches the chart's own baseline‑1.00 identity column, see §5.
    Airframe variants Fig 3A‑22, 3A‑23 Refusal speed uses Fig 3A‑21 (C‑130H / KC‑130H). Rosemount‑probe aircraft (3A‑22) and C‑130H‑30 (3A‑23) read from their own sheets and give different numbers. (Fig 3A‑24, the adjustments chart, is not an airframe variant — it is now implemented in §1.1 and applies to all three.)
    Three‑engine cases Fig 3A‑97, 3A‑98, 3A‑73–3A‑88 Everything here is four engines, 50 % flaps. Three‑engine acceleration check time, three‑engine take‑off and flaps‑up data are separate chart families.
